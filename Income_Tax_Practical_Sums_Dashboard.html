Meaning & Types of Taxes

Chapter 1 · Unit I

Tax = a compulsory financial charge imposed by the government on income, goods, services, or transactions, with no direct quid-pro-quo (you don't get a specific benefit back in exchange). It funds public spending — infrastructure, defence, welfare, administration.

Incidence & impactFall on the same personCan be shifted to another person

Capital vs Revenue — Receipts & Expenses

Chapter 1 · Unit I
TypeMeaningExamples
Capital ReceiptNot part of normal trading operations — sale of a capital asset or capital infusion. Not taxable as income (may attract Capital Gains separately)Sale of fixed asset, owner's capital, a loan raised
Revenue ReceiptArises from the normal course of business/employment — taxable as incomeSale of stock, fees, rent, interest, commission, salary
Capital ExpenditureAcquires/improves a fixed asset, or gives enduring benefit beyond the year — capitalised, depreciated over timeMachinery purchase, goodwill, building addition
Revenue ExpenditureDay-to-day running cost — fully deductible in the year incurredSalaries, rent, raw material, repairs
4 tests to tell them apart
  • Nature of asset — fixed asset (capital) vs current/circulating asset (revenue)
  • Enduring benefit — benefit lasts beyond the year (capital) vs consumed within the year (revenue)
  • Purpose — connected to regular earning activity (revenue) vs a one-off structural change (capital)
  • Recurring nature — recurring items lean revenue; one-time substantial items lean capital

Gifts — Taxability

Chapter 1 · Unit I

A gift from a relative is always fully exempt, any amount. Otherwise:

Type of giftTaxable when
Monetary giftExceeds ₹50,000 (from a non-relative) → taxable IN FULL under "Income from Other Sources"
Immovable property, no considerationStamp duty value > ₹50,000 → taxable
Immovable property, inadequate consideration(Stamp Duty Value − Consideration Paid) > ₹50,000 → the difference is taxable
Movable property, no considerationFair Market Value (FMV) > ₹50,000 → taxable
Movable property, inadequate consideration(FMV − Consideration Paid) > ₹50,000 → the difference is taxable

If the value does NOT exceed ₹50,000 in any case above, it is not taxable at all.

In plain terms: surcharge is an "extra tax on the tax", charged only once your Total Income crosses ₹50 lakh — it does not apply to most student-level exam problems, but always check the total income figure first.

Health & Education Cess (HEC)

4% of (Tax + Surcharge). Cess funds health & education schemes and applies to EVERY taxpayer, at every income level — there's no threshold like surcharge has. Formula: Cess = 4% × (Income Tax + Surcharge).

Rebate — Sec. 87A

Available to resident individuals only, if Total Income ≤ ₹12,00,000. Rebate = lower of ₹60,000 or the tax actually calculated on Total Income. Effect: tax becomes fully NIL for resident individuals with Total Income up to ₹12,00,000 (before cess).

Order of computation — memorise this sequence
  1. Compute tax slab-by-slab on Total Income
  2. Less: Rebate u/s 87A (if Total Income ≤ ₹12,00,000)
  3. Add: Surcharge (only if Total Income > ₹50,00,000)
  4. Add: Health & Education Cess @ 4% on (Tax − Rebate + Surcharge)
  5. = Final Tax Liability
⚠ Exam trap: rebate is applied BEFORE cess, and cess is calculated on tax AFTER rebate/surcharge — never apply cess on the slab tax before deducting the rebate.

Residential Status — Rules (Sec. 6)

Chapter 1 · Unit I
Basic Conditions (need at least 1)Additional Conditions (for OR)
1) Stay of 182 days or more in the PY1) Resident in 2 of the 10 PYs preceding the relevant PY
2) 60 days in the current PY AND 365 days in the 4 PYs preceding it2) Stay of 730 days or more in the 7 PYs preceding the relevant PY
Category rules
  • Ordinary Resident (OR): 1 basic condition + BOTH additional conditions
  • Non-Ordinary Resident (NOR): 1 basic condition + no additional condition (or only 1)
  • Non-Resident (NR): no basic condition satisfied at all
Special provisions
  • Not an Indian citizen / not of Indian origin, earns > ₹15,00,000 Indian income, stays 120–181 days → must pay Indian tax
  • Person of Indian origin abroad, visits India, Indian-source income > ₹15,00,000 → Resident if stay ≥ 120 days
  • Indian citizen leaving India for a job, or as crew of an Indian ship (govt.-approved) → "60 days" is replaced by "182 days" in condition 2
⚠ Exam trap: distractors like "didn't stay more than 15 days at one place" or "kept a dwelling maintained in India" do NOT matter — Indian residential status depends purely on total days physically present in India.

Chargeability — Basis of Charge (Sec. 15)

Chapter 2 · Unit II

Salary is taxed on "due" or "receipt" basis, whichever is earlier:

  • Salary due from an employer in the PY, whether actually paid or not
  • Salary paid/allowed in the PY, even if not yet due, or already due before the PY
  • Arrears of salary paid in the PY, if not already taxed in an earlier year

Effect: salary is taxed exactly once — the year it becomes due, or the year it's received, whichever comes first. This prevents double taxation of the same amount.

General perquisite rules
  • Fixed Medical Allowance — always fully taxable, even if fully spent on medical care
